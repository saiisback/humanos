# Judge journey

1. Open the single chat workspace on desktop and phone. Inspect Connections. Missing credentials must appear unavailable.
2. Sign in with a JAW passkey account. Inspect the backend-authenticated account in Identity. Cancel one sign-in attempt to show that no session is issued.
3. Request IDKit Proof of Human at the root-creation trust gate. Complete verification and show the account-bound root. Cancel a separate attempt to show that the JAW session remains but agent creation stays locked.
4. Describe the ETHGlobal Tokyo participant/travel application in the composer. Review the proposed mandate and expiry in the conversation.
5. Authorize exact capabilities and register the task agent on ENSv2 Sepolia. Show that the JAW account owns the new root while scoped registrar and agent roles govern lifecycle and records. Wait for the finalized authorization snapshot.
6. If a server-reviewed onchain permission is available, inspect its exact target/function, allowance, spender and expiry before opening JAW. Show the server-recorded status and revocation. An unverified grant cannot authorize execution.
7. Run the Flue agent. Inspect the proposed application payload, Jev assessment and separate HumanOS policy decision in the transcript.
8. Request fresh action-bound World approval. Inspect the digest and expiry, approve, execute, and show the persisted receipt. Repeat execution to demonstrate idempotency. If ENS publication is pending, retry publication without repeating the external effect.
9. Prepare the calendar event, explicitly confirm the consequential effect, execute, and show mission completion.
10. Repeat with cancellation, expiry and revoked ENS authority; verify refusal and absence of a new side effect. Restart Flue after durable admission and confirm recovery without duplicate effects.

Browser videos/screenshots from synthetic test transports must be labelled non-qualifying. This repository's application and calendar destination are development services, not external organizer/calendar accounts.
